The Studio

About

What we do

Afterburner Games is an independent project focused on understanding how systems work and making games that are enjoyable to spend time in. The technical foundation draws from engineers who had to accomplish a lot with limited hardware — the late-1990s and early-2000s console and PC era in particular.

Good engineering can make surprisingly little hardware do a lot of work. That idea drives the engines and tools being built here, including engines designed to run games directly in the browser.

The target period

The design vocabulary comes from roughly 1998–2006: PlayStation 2, Xbox, GameCube, Dreamcast, and early programmable PC graphics. Chunky geometry, deliberate rendering choices, distinctive animation, compact game loops, and straightforward interfaces.

Modern hardware and distribution are available. The design and engineering character of that period is deliberately retained.

Not nostalgia for its own sake

This is not a museum and not an emulator. It is a modern attempt to continue the kind of game development that produced the games that still feel good to play years later — readable systems, characterful presentation, and software that respects its hardware.

Engines

Core Technology

The primary technical goal is the expansion of two proprietary engines. Both are informed by the deliberate decisions about memory, rendering, animation, visibility and simulation found in engines such as LithTech, id Tech, RenderWare and the proprietary console engines of the fifth- and sixth-generation era.

Axiom2.5D
Isometric / 2.5D Engine

Proprietary isometric engine aimed at strategy and simulation. Prioritises clear visibility, compact asset use, and the rendering and simulation trade-offs that made early-2000s isometric games readable and performant. Currently powers "Dope Wars - King of the Street".

Active Development
Axiom3D
Browser-Based 3D Engine

New browser-capable 3D engine whose design vocabulary draws from early-2000s engines. Built to support a larger real-time strategy game while remaining practical to ship and play without installation. Modern hardware, older design discipline.

Principles

How we work

Games that leave room to breathe

Games can be difficult and tense. They should also give the player space. Sit down, manage something, explore, or simply mess around. Tension works better when recovery exists. Not every minute needs notifications, objectives and reward loops.

Games should be enjoyable to spend time in.

No microtransactions

If you buy a game, you get the game. No virtual currencies, loot boxes, battle passes, or content deliberately withheld to be sold later. Releases are payware or donationware. That is the entire relationship.

Mechanical Sympathy

Design around hardware. Cache, bandwidth and allocation strategy before clever algorithms.

Browser as Platform

Useful constraints on memory and distribution. Instant play without installers or large downloads.

Character over Spectacle

Chunky geometry, distinctive animation, compact loops. Technical compromises that become personality.
